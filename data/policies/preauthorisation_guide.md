# Pre-authorisation Guide

Pre-authorisation is our written confirmation, before treatment, that the treatment is covered and of the
amount we expect to pay. It applies to all plans.

## Treatments that Need Pre-authorisation

You or your hospital must obtain pre-authorisation for:

- every planned (non-emergency) inpatient admission;
- day surgery where the estimated cost exceeds SGD 5,000;
- MRI, CT and PET scans;
- all cancer treatment, including chemotherapy, radiotherapy and targeted or biologic therapies;
- bariatric surgery (subject to the General Exclusions);
- physiotherapy beyond the first 6 sessions.

## How to Request Pre-authorisation

The hospital or treating doctor submits the pre-authorisation form through the provider portal, together with
the provisional diagnosis (ICD-10 code), the proposed procedure, the expected length of stay and an itemised
cost estimate.

For planned treatment, the request must reach us at least 5 working days before the admission date.

For emergency admissions, the hospital must notify us within 48 hours of admission. Emergency treatment is
never delayed while we review the request.

## Response Times

We respond to standard requests within 2 working days of receiving a complete request. Requests marked
urgent by the treating doctor are answered within 4 hours, 24 hours a day.

A pre-authorisation is valid for 30 days from the date it is issued. If the admission is postponed beyond
30 days, a new request is needed.

## If You Do Not Obtain Pre-authorisation

If treatment that needs pre-authorisation goes ahead without it, we will still assess the claim, but you
will pay an additional 20% co-payment on the eligible expenses for that treatment, on top of any deductible
and co-insurance. This penalty does not apply to emergency admissions notified within 48 hours.

## Pre-authorisation Is Not a Guarantee

Pre-authorisation confirms cover based on the information provided. If the final treatment, diagnosis or
charges differ materially from the request, we will reassess the claim against the policy terms.
